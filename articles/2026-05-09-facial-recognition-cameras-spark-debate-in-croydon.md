---
title: Facial recognition cameras spark debate in Croydon
image: /images/uploads/image-1.jpeg
---
Live facial recognition technology being used by the Metropolitan Police in Croydon has sparked fresh debate over privacy and public safety.

The technology scans faces in public areas and compares them against watchlists of wanted suspects or missing persons. Police say the system has already helped identify several individuals linked to crimes across London.

Croydon has become one of the main testing areas for the technology due to its large crowds and busy town centre. Supporters argue the cameras help officers catch dangerous offenders faster and improve public safety.

However, civil liberties groups and some residents have criticised the expansion of facial recognition, warning that innocent people are being scanned without consent. Campaigners have also raised concerns about possible mistakes and the storage of biometric data.

The Metropolitan Police insist the system is only used in specific locations for limited periods of time and that images of people not matched on watchlists are immediately deleted.

Local residents in Croydon remain divided. Some say the technology could help reduce theft, violence and antisocial behaviour, while others believe it represents a major increase in surveillance in everyday life.

The debate comes as police forces across the UK continue expanding the use of artificial intelligence and surveillance technology in public spaces.
